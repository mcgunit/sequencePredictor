# Scoring: hits, profit, prize tables and the ranking

> Moved out of the README on 5 Nov 2026, word for word; see "Out of date" at the end of this page for what the 5 Nov check found.

## Joker+ (`jokerplus`)

Joker+ is a 6-digit positional game (digits 0-9 drawn *with replacement* - 86% of draws repeat a digit, leading zeros count) plus a zodiac sign from 12. It is modeled as a positional game with one **categorical special column**: the sign is encoded to a code 0-11 at every CSV parse site (`Helpers.encode_zodiac`/`decode_zodiac`, canonical regulation order, accepting the CSV spelling `Tweeling`, the regulation's `Tweelingen` and the English names the live API returns - `DataFetcher` parses the API's `'430109Scorpio'` form and writes the Dutch CSV spelling), so every model, backtest and report works on integers; the UI decodes it back to the name. All positional machinery generalizes to 6 slots (statistical pick3-capable set via `run_model_with_special_column`, per-position boosting, DL per-position softmax with a 12-class sign head, the positional meta-learner with a sign `special_model` - so the classical and quantum meta rows exist for Joker+ too). The per-slot vote rows (`WeightedEnsemble Model` / `SubsetEnsemble Model`, roadmap item 7) exist for Joker+ but stay switched off unless `"useJokerplusEnsemble": true` is set in `bestParams_jokerplus.json`, and `RL Ticket Model` is deliberately absent - **the player cannot choose the digits** (combinations are system-generated; only the sign is selectable), so digit "ticket construction" would be fiction for this game. That also frames the research value: Joker+ cannot be made player-profitable through digit prediction - any digit predictability found here is an integrity/countermeasure signal - while the sign is the one real lever (its own tier only refunds the stake). Hyperopt covers it in the statistical, boosting, deep-learning and quantum tuners with profit per bet as the objective (`HyperoptQuantum` scores the per-slot argmax digits only, sign unknown).

## Hit counting & profit semantics

Hits are counted **per pool**, never as one flat set intersection over the whole result row (a predicted main equal to a star's numeric value is not a hit - the pools are separate drawings):

- **Euromillions / EuroDreams / VikingLotto**: main numbers score only against the drawn mains, the special column(s) (stars / dream number / super viking) only against the drawn specials. Displayed as **`N (M)`** = N main hits, M special hits, with green cell highlighting per pool.
- **Lotto** follows the real game's tiers: a play is 6 numbers scored against the 6 drawn mains, and the 7th drawn value (the bonus) only ever *supplements* a partial match - `5 (1)` (5 mains + bonus, amber cell) is a high tier but not the jackpot; `6 (0)` is the jackpot, and a full main match makes a bonus match mathematically impossible since the bonus differs from every drawn main. The bonus is matched against the played numbers themselves (a play has no bonus slot).
- **Keno / Pick3**: unchanged (single pool; Pick3 positional).
- **Joker+** follows its regulation (Reglement Joker+, Sept 2023): prizes go to groups of *consecutive* positional matches counted from the **left** end or the **right** end of the 6-digit number, plus the zodiac sign. Hits are therefore tracked as **`L/R (Z)`** - leading run / trailing run / sign (e.g. `3/1 (1)`); a full match is `6/0`. The UI colors leading-run cells green, trailing-run cells blue and a matching sign amber. "hits" in the report and lag analysis is L+R.

Games are classified once as **positional** (`Helpers.is_positional_game`: Pick3 and Joker+ - drawn order kept, digits may repeat, per-position models, no set-style rows such as MarkovBayesian/multi-label boosting - the vote ensembles take a per-slot form there) or set-based; every positional branch keys on that predicate, not on a game name.

This split runs through everything: the day-view highlights and `N (M)` hit column, `matchingNumbers` in the day JSONs (`matching_numbers` = mains, `special_matching_numbers` = specials/bonus), the match counts of the draw and history views (`draws.js`), `modelPerformance.json`'s `avg_hits` (mains only) and `avg_special_hits`, the lag analysis, the randomness watch, and `src/Backtester.py`'s per-day hit metrics and baselines (which now bet main-sized tickets) feeding every hyperopt objective.

Profit is **net profit per 1 EUR-stake convention** in both payout games. `pick3_ticket_profit` implements the official Belgian Pick-3 rules (Reglement Pick-3 juli 2024): the tracked ticket plays all four bet types (straight, box, front pair, back pair - 4 EUR stake; triples cannot play box, 3 EUR) and prizes cumulate, so an exact hit nets +676 (distinct digits) or +756 (with a double), including the 1 EUR units-digit consolation where applicable. `keno_ticket_profit` returns payout minus the 1 EUR stake (it previously returned gross wins and double-counted the stake on losses), so `profit_per_bet` is comparable across games. `jokerplus_ticket_profit` implements Art. 27 of the Joker+ regulation: stake 1.50 EUR; all 6 digits -> 20,000 EUR (200,000 EUR fixed-minimum jackpot with the sign); otherwise `T[L] + T[R]` with `T = {1: 2, 2: 5, 3: 20, 4: 200, 5: 2000}` (left and right groups cumulate) plus 1.50 EUR for a matching sign.

## Profit for Lotto, EuroMillions, EuroDreams and Vikinglotto: each draw's prize table

Since 3 Nov 2026 (the owner's request) these three games are scored in euro too, like keno, pick3 and Joker+. Their prizes are pari-mutuel, so there is no fixed table: the National Lottery's draw API - the same call `src/DataFetcher.py` reads the numbers from - carries every draw's prize tiers (name, the amount per winner, the Belgian winners), back to at least 2015. `src/PrizeTables.py` keeps them per game in `data/trainingData/<game>/<game>-prizes.json` (`{date: {price, jackpot, tiers}}`): the daily fetch adds the new draws' tables and `Predictor.py` fills in, once, the tables of every draw the game has scored tickets for. A row's profit on a draw is **one grid** of its own ticket at the draw's base price (a ticket without its full special column - the RL Ticket rows before 3 Nov 2026 - has no bet on that draw, hits only; Lotto 1.50 EUR: the six numbers - the multi-pick's 7th-9th stay a hit-rate study, and the statistical rows of 8 Nov 2025 - 13 May 2026 stored a 7th value, a guess at the bonus column that no grid plays, which the one-time `TrimLottoBonusGuess.py` cuts from the day files - the owner's decision of 3 Nov 2026, with a backup; until it has run they play their first six; EuroMillions 2.50 EUR: five numbers and two stars; EuroDreams 2.50 EUR: six numbers and the dream number; Vikinglotto 2.00 EUR, since 5 Nov 2026: six numbers and the viking number) - the tier its hits reach (Lotto's bonus counts when one of the six is it) minus the price. A tier nobody won shows 0 in the API (the money rolls over), so a hit on it is valued at the draw's announced jackpot for the top tier, otherwise at that tier's median over the stored draws where it paid; EuroDreams' two annuities count at their rule value (20,000 EUR a month for 30 years, 2,000 a month for 5 years); the raffles (Lucky Lotto, My Bonus) are drawn on a code and are not scored. `Helpers.generate_model_performance_report` then ranks these games on the capped profit bound like the payout games (each bet capped at 20 EUR, mean minus one standard error), so one jackpot cannot carry a row; a draw without a table has no bet, and a game without any table ranks on average hits as before. What stays on hits for now: the combination search and its shuffled control (it would need every ticket scored against every other draw's table), and the tuners and meta-learners. The History pages show the profit next to the hits since 5 Nov 2026 (`draws.js` PRIZE_GAMES, the same scoring ticket for ticket: each ticket's profit of one grid, a draw's and a month's profit over the grids its table could score; a ticket that is not a full grid or a draw without a table shows no profit). **Vikinglotto** joined on 5 Nov 2026, once the owner confirmed its tiers: 'k' is k numbers without the viking number, 'k+Fav' k numbers with it (Fav is the viking number), '6/' all six whatever the viking number (the jackpot); one correct number already pays 2.00 EUR, the price back. The winner counts fit those odds (checked on four draws, 4 Nov 2026). Its tables fill in on the first daily run after the deploy; until then it ranks on hits. The users' Home summary shows the best row by money for the four games. `python3 -m src.PrizeTables` (in `npm test`) checks the tier reading and the scoring.

## Lotto multi-pick (system play): the 7th to 9th numbers

Since 3 Oct 2026 (the owner's proposal) every **Lotto** row still predicts six numbers, but two things changed in how they are shown and what else a row carries. **Order:** a lotto row that carries extras lists its six numbers in the order of the model's own probability, highest first - not small to large as before; a set game's hits are set hits, so nothing in the scoring moves, only the reading does (the one position-sensitive reader of a ticket, the RL Ticket Model's rank feature, reads every ticket sorted ascending since this change, so its stored policy keeps the meaning it was trained on). **Three extra numbers:** each row names, after its six, the three next most probable numbers it did *not* play (`multiPick` in the day file; `src/MultiPick.py`, applied in `Predictor.py` at the end of each row-producing stage from the row's own `{number: score}` ranking - the statistical and boosting rows' `score_numbers`, the deep-learning rows' per-number softmax maximum, the meta rows' probabilities; the vote rows rank by their weighted votes, the RL ticket by the scores of its decode; `HybridStatisticalModel` has no `score_numbers` and a row whose ranking fails that run gets none - those keep their six small to large and show empty shaded cells). For the sampling rows (Markov, the Monte Carlo rows, PoissonMarkov) the ranking is the model's own distribution estimated by its `score_numbers` sampling, a larger sample than the single ticket it played, so a played number can rank below an unplayed one: the six stay the ticket, the extras are the best of the rest. When the game has a meta-learner artifact (`data/models/<game>/meta_learner*.joblib`, weekly from `TrainMetaLearner.py`) the rankings of the base rows come free from the scoring that artifact needs; without one the step computes them itself and says so in the log (about ten seconds a day). They are shown as three shaded, dashed cells after the six, for a **system play** as Belgian Lotto sells it: 7 numbers = 7 grids = 10.50 EUR, 8 numbers = 28 grids = 42 EUR, 9 numbers = 84 grids = 126 EUR, at 1.50 EUR a grid against one grid for the six. The hits column shows the hits of the six and, after a dot, the hits among all nine; the day list shows the best nine-number hits of the day; the day files keep the six as the ticket and the three as `multiPick`, so every existing reader of a ticket is untouched. **The other number games (2 Nov 2026, the owner's request):** EuroMillions, EuroDreams, Vikinglotto and Keno keep the same order - each row's mains most probable first, from the same rankings at the same places (`src/MultiPick.PROBABILITY_ORDER`, `in_score_order`), the stars, the dream number and the viking where they were, every keno ticket and subset ordered - without extras; the positional games (pick3, Joker+, the markets) keep their slots. The order is the reading the owner uses to see whether the lower-rated places hit about as often as the top ones (what a fair game predicts). A deep-learning row ranks by its main positions only, since a star's softmax shares the labels 1-12 with the mains. The day files before 2 Nov keep those games' tickets small to large. The users' Home shows each game's top 5 tickets in this stored order, lotto's 7th-9th shaded after the six.

**The research question, and why the raw numbers must not be read.** More numbers win more by arithmetic alone - exact hypergeometric, 6 of 45 drawn: any 6 numbers hit 3 or more of the six drawn mains - the smallest prize that needs no bonus number; the ranks that use the bonus number are not scored here - 2.38% of the time, any 7 numbers 3.94%, 8 numbers 5.94%, 9 numbers 8.39% - 3.5 times the chance at 84 times the price - and any single extra number is drawn 6/45 = 13.3% of the time. So "do nine model numbers hit more than six" has a known answer before any data. The questions the tracking answers are: *does the model's 7th, 8th or 9th number hit more often than 13.3%*, and *do nine model numbers reach the smallest prize more often than 8.39%* - against *those* levels, over many draws. `Helpers.generate_model_performance_report` scores both per row (`multi_pick`: over the draws with extras, the average hits of the six and of the nine, the share of draws with 3 or more main hits with six and with nine, the hit rate of each extra number) and carries the chance table per game; Admin › Models shows them in lotto's *Multi-pick* section, tagged when above the level, with the same caveat as every other card: one row above a level is noise over a few draws, a row that stays above it for many draws is the finding.

**Steps (roadmap item 10).** 1 - built: the extras, the ordering, the scoring against chance. 2 - after about fifty lotto draws (spring 2027 at two draws a week), read the extra-number hit rates with a binomial interval against 13.3% and the nine-number win rate against 8.39%, row by row and pooled over the rows; a row whose extras clear the level while its six do not says the ranking's tail carries what its head lacks. 3 - only then rethink the first six: if the 7th-9th numbers of a row hit more than its 4th-6th, the ticket construction (softmax sampling, voted tickets) is throwing probability away and an argmax-by-ranking ticket is the candidate; test it on the backtest before it is served. 4 - the system play's money: Lotto's prizes are pari-mutuel per draw, so a euro value needs the published prize per rank per draw - a fetch to add to the data layer before any "profit of a 9-number play" is shown; until then the pages show hits and chance, not money.

## Which draw a prediction is for

A day JSON is named after the draw it scored, and its `newPrediction` is the prediction for the **next** draw of that game - the rows that reappear as the following file's `currentPrediction`. The coming draw's table (Home, the Lottery pages, the newest draw in Admin › Games) is therefore labelled with the draw it applies to. The date is derived from the game's own stored history rather than a hardcoded calendar (`nextDrawDate` / `nextDrawDay` in `draws.js`): the weekdays of the last 40 draws up to that file are the game's schedule, and the prediction applies to the first later date falling on one of them - so a schedule change is picked up by itself, and an older page is read with the schedule of its own time. Checked against the full stored history, this reproduces the draw that actually followed in 765 of 767 cases; the two misses are a missing 31 December file for the two daily games, i.e. a gap in the stored data rather than a wrong schedule. A date already past says so ("that day has passed; the next run scores it"): the newest stored prediction is then for a draw that has already taken place, which happens when the predictor has not run since (for example while the weekly hyperopt still holds `process.lock`).

## Model performance report (the History ranking)

After each prediction run, `Predictor.py` writes `data/database/modelPerformance.json` (`Helpers.generate_model_performance_report`): per game, every model's record over **all scored history** — average hits of the main ticket, best day, scored-draw count, and (for Keno/Pick3, which have real payout tables) total profit and average profit per bet. The administrator reads it on *Best model & rankings* (`/app/admin/models`, `/api/admin/models`): a "Best model per game" card, and per game the full model ranking; a user sees the first five rows of the same ranking (`ranking.js`). Keno/Pick3/Joker+ rank by the **profit bound** since 13 Oct 2026 - per draw the profit per bet with every bet capped at the 20 EUR lucky-strike threshold (a keno bet against a fair ticket of its size, so 0 is a fair ticket), its mean minus one standard error over the draws: `src/TuningScore`'s capped profit bound (`Helpers._profit_bound`) - the objective the statistical, boosting and RL ticket tuners score keno by; for pick3 and Joker+ those tuners score slot hits (the RL ticket tuner plain hits for Joker+) and this bound is at most their 0.001-weighted tie-breaker (*The objective and the gate*) - with the raw profit per bet in its own column; until then they ranked by the raw profit per bet, which one winning ticket decides (on 13 Oct a 65-bet keno row with one 9-ticket at 7 of 9 led a 513-bet one, and pick3's best was one straight). Other games rank by average hits — per-bet/per-draw averages rather than totals, since models joined the tracking at different times. Models with fewer scored draws than `minDrawsForRanking` (10, or the max available if lower) are listed but marked as a short record and sorted below the ranked ones, so a two-day-old model can't claim "best" off one lucky draw. Since 27 Sept 2026 the card also carries the **forward record**: a `since.json` at the repository root (`{"since": "2026-09-27", "label": "September 2026 design freeze"}`, `src/Since.py`) makes the report add, per game, the same ranking counted only over the stored days on or after that date (`games[game].since`, same metric, same minimum-draws guard), and the card shows the best row since the freeze next to the all-history one, with each model's since value and draw count in the game's ranking (a game reads "no scored draw since ... yet" until its first draw on or after the date is scored; no band was ever applied to the since column, because the best of thirty rows over a handful of draws clears a band measured over long histories by luck alone, and since 27 Oct 2026 none labels any column - *Q0 - the per-row chance test*). Nothing is rebuilt and no file is touched: the date is a filter that turns the record after a design freeze into that design's own out-of-sample track record - the forward counterpart of the lockbox. Not every stored day was predicted before its draw, so since 27 Oct 2026 the since record counts the live record only (next paragraph; a game whose liveness could not be judged falls back to the stored rows). A malformed `since.json` costs the since ranking, never the report. The same report also feeds the *Phase-shift* page (tracked lag peaks, next section) and the *Randomness watch* page (entropy/KL/autoencoder-anomaly monitoring, see the security research section).

**The live record (27 Oct 2026, `src/Liveness.py`, `src/drawClose.json`).** History rebuilds and late runs wrote lottery tickets after the sales close, and some tickets were rewritten after the draw, so the all-draws record counts tickets nobody could have played. A row's draw now counts in its **live record** only when its ticket - the row in the `newPrediction` of the day file before the draw - was saved before that draw's sales close. `src/drawClose.json` holds the close per game and weekday in Brussels time: the National Lottery API's `drawTime`, wagers stopping one second before it (lotto, EuroDreams, keno and pick3 checked over a year, EuroMillions, Viking Lotto and Joker+ over six weeks). A ticket is judged in this order: **live by the stamp** (the file's `madeAt` is before the close and the first commit after the stamp holds the ticket), **live by a commit** (a commit before the close holds it), **recovered** (the version written by the latest commit before the close holds the row with other content - the ticket was rewritten after the draw, and the live record scores it as it stood at the close), else **back-built or late**. A second row of one name on a draw is not live, and a file's modification time is never evidence. Git is trusted only for the checkout's own `data/database` in a full (non-shallow) clone, under one 60 s deadline for the whole scan. Without it a stamped ticket file is judged by its stamp and an unstamped draw by the previous `lotteryLiveness.json` up to its `checkedThrough`; with neither the game reads *unchecked* - every row counts as not live, the admin pages show its draws without a live split, and it is never ranked on its live record, so a one-day git failure never becomes the users' history. The report adds per row `live_draws` and `live` (the same figures over the live record), per game `live` (a ranking shaped like `since`), `liveness` (how it was judged, the counts, and the rewrite audit: hits of the tickets at the close against hits of the rewritten ones) and `rankedOn: "live"`: the lottery games are **ranked on the live record** (same metric, the 10-draw guard on live draws), and with them the users' Top 5 and the point-in-time History ranking (`rankhistory.js`, `CACHE_VERSION` 2); the admin pages show every stored draw's value beside it, and the combination search keeps the all-draws order. The per-draw verdicts go to `data/database/lotteryLiveness.json` (sorted keys, only the draws with a row that is not live as stored, rewritten only when a verdict changes, committed by the daily run). On the pages: the admin draw table marks each such row (back-built or late, rewritten - with the ticket that stood at the close, scored - or duplicate); the users' history greys a draw whose five included a ticket made after the sales close and leaves it out of the totals ("over N draws (M left out: tickets made after the sales close)"); and the coming draw shows its sales close ("sales close 13:00"), with a warning when its tickets were made after it. The point-in-time cut-off of `rankhistory.js` is 15:00 Brussels time, or the draw's sales close when that is earlier (Sunday keno, pick3 and Joker+ at 13:00). Measured on production's history of 26 Oct 2026, 12,170 scored row-draws: 7,301 live as stored, 2,018 rewritten after the draw (recovered), 2,851 back-built or late; 502 of 1,124 draws fully live as stored; no back-built or late draw since 10 Sep 2026. Ranking on it changes the best row of Viking Lotto (MarkovBayesian to MetaLearner, on exactly the 10 live draws the guard asks), keno (QuantumVQC to HybridStatisticalModel) and Joker+ (MetaLearner to LightGBM). Not on the live record yet: the combination search, the phase-shift analysis, `HyperoptEnsemble.py`, `HyperoptRLTicket.py` / `src/RLTicketModel.py` and `src/RLPositionModel.py` still read every stored ticket. The market games keep their day-level check (`Helpers._days_predicted_before_draw`). Self-check: `python3 -m src.Liveness` (in `npm test`).

Next to the single-row ranking, each game carries a `combinations` section (`Helpers._build_combination_report`, roadmap item 2), shown as the "Best combination per game" card: every pair and triple of the ranked rows is scored on the draws all of its members were scored on (the same `minDrawsForRanking` floor). Keno/Pick3/Joker+ rank sets by **net profit per draw** of playing every member's tickets - profit is additive, so a set beats its best row only when at least two rows are positive on those draws, and the card states whether it does; a greedy build-up beyond the best triple keeps adding rows while profit per draw improves. (Profit per *bet*, the single-row metric, is a stake-weighted mean of the members' and can never beat the best member; it is shown, not ranked on.) The hit-scored games rank by the **best line held per draw**: the mean over draws of the best member's hits, the quantity a player holding several lines cares about, which rewards rows whose good days do not coincide - it grows with every extra line, so pairs and triples are ranked separately and no greedy build-up is run. Because hundreds of combinations are scored, the card shows how many were evaluated and a **shuffled-history control**: the identical search re-run 100 times with each draw's real result moved to another day (every ticket keeps its day), which gives the distribution of the best combination *by luck alone* and a p-value - the share of shuffles whose best combination did at least as well. Ticket-versus-result scores are computed once (one-hot matrix products for hits and Keno, whose payout depends only on played and matched counts; the positional payout functions for Pick3/Joker+), so the whole section adds under two seconds to the daily run. At the time of writing (2026-09-14) no game's p-value is anywhere near small: the card shows the winner of a lottery among combinations, which is exactly what it is meant to make visible.

## Phase-shift (lag) analysis

Also part of the report (Admin › *Phase-shift*): every day's `newPrediction` is scored not only against the draw it was made for (**lag +1**) but against the thirty draws that follow. If a model's signal were real but time-shifted, its average hits would peak consistently at some lag > 1; a flat curve across all lags means the hits come from draw-independent number-frequency structure, not timing. Pick3 is scored positionally (digit in the right place, chance level 0.3 per draw). Interpret peaks against the row's overall spread and sample size — with under ~100 scored draws per lag, the "best lag" bounces around by chance; a real phase shift would show the *same* peak lag persistently across time (and plausibly across related models), not a one-off maximum. That persistence check is automated: each run keeps only its single best peak per model and appends it to `data/database/lagPeakHistory.json` (one entry per run date, last 60 runs); the UI shows this run's peak with a z-score against the model's own lag profile, the most frequent peak across runs (highlighted once ≥3 runs and ≥50% agree), and a run-length-encoded chronological peak trail (`+30×5` = holding still, `+26 → +28 → +30` = drifting).
