## 4. Same models for crypto and shares - a predictor, not a trading bot (large, own phase plan)

Research question: *do the principles that fail to find structure in a fair lottery find any in market prices?* - the same statistical, deep-learning, boosting and quantum model families, the same walk-forward evaluation, the same negative controls. Not a trading bot: the output is tracked predictions and honest scoring.

**Decisions taken:** storage moves from day JSONs to **SQLite inside this repo** (the repo already carries `db.sqlite3` for Optuna; one more file, no new service), and the cadence is **daily bars** - one "draw" per trading day keeps parity with the lottery framing and the daily cron rhythm.

Design sketch:
- **Data sources (answer to "is there an open API?")**: yes, for daily bars there are free, keyless options. Crypto: Binance's public REST endpoint `GET /api/v3/klines` (1d candles, no key, generous limits) with CoinGecko's free API as the fallback and as the source of the market-cap ranking (keyless, roughly 10-30 requests per minute, `/coins/{id}/market_chart`). Shares: Stooq's daily CSV download (`https://stooq.com/q/d/l/?s=nvda.us&i=d`, no key, end-of-day, Euronext tickers with a suffix such as `asml.nl`) with `yfinance` (unofficial Yahoo Finance) as the fallback - *both failed the probe of 2026-09-30, see M1 below; Nasdaq's own historical endpoint is what runs*; keyed free tiers such as Alpha Vantage (25 requests/day) or Twelve Data are alternatives if either breaks. Free equity data is end-of-day and for personal/research use, which is exactly this use; nothing here needs a paid feed. One fetch per instrument per day is far below every limit. Stored in `data/markets.sqlite3` (tables: instruments, bars, predictions, results, model_performance).
- **Universe (decision): start small and fixed.** Crypto: the top 5 coins by market cap at kickoff (BTC, ETH and the next three at that date), quoted in USDT; shares: a handful of liquid names for testing, NVIDIA first, plus e.g. AAPL, MSFT, ASML. The list is frozen in the `instruments` table for an evaluation window and only changed between windows - re-selecting "the top 5" every day would smuggle survivorship and look-ahead into the results.
- **Daily tracking, exactly like the lottery games**: every day each model writes its prediction per instrument (bin, direction, confidence) to `predictions`; the next trading day's bar settles it - hit (exact bin / adjacent bin / direction) and the paper P&L of the fixed rule with fees go to `results`, and `model_performance` ranks the rows over the scored history with the same `minDrawsForRanking` guard and the same "compared with the result the day after" semantics the History page has for draws. Crypto trades seven days a week, shares five: settlement is per instrument at its next available bar, so a Friday share prediction settles on Monday and the sequence a model sees has no artificial holiday rows.
- **Models to check, market-specific: GARCH.** A GARCH(1,1) (the `arch` package) with a zero or AR(1) mean forecasts the next day's conditional variance; the resulting predictive distribution of the return gives the probability of every return bin directly, so it is a proper probabilistic baseline row ("GARCH Model") next to the lottery models, and its variance forecast is a volatility-regime feature the other rows can consume. The expected outcome is instructive for the research question: GARCH predicts *volatility* well and *direction* not at all, so any bin accuracy above 1/K it shows comes from volatility clustering (the extreme bins), which is the known predictable component of returns - a reference for judging what the lottery-born models find.
- **Models to check, market-specific: Regime HMM** (from arXiv [2603.04441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04441), "Explainable Regime-Aware Investing", Boukardagha, Feb 2026). The paper's machinery is a strictly causal Gaussian hidden Markov model refit daily on an expanding window of per-asset features (daily log return, 60-day rolling volatility, 20-day mean return, all built from bars up to *t-1*), with the number of regimes reselected periodically by a complexity-penalised one-step-ahead predictive log-likelihood, and each fitted regime matched to a persistent "template" by the closed-form 2-Wasserstein distance between Gaussians so regime identities stay stable across refits (no label switching). In the paper those regime probabilities feed a mean-variance allocator; that layer is the trading bot this track does not build, so it is left out. **How it becomes a row (`Regime HMM Model`)**: per instrument, slice the instrument's return coordinate out of each regime's Gaussian, weight the regimes by the one-step-ahead state distribution (last filtered posterior propagated through the transition matrix) and integrate that Gaussian mixture over the K quantile bins - bin by bin, not through a single moment-matched Gaussian, because the mixture shape is exactly where the regime information lives - giving the per-position bin probabilities every markets row emits; the dominant template is recorded with the prediction so the History page can show which regime the model believed it was in. One HMM per market group (crypto trades seven days, shares five, so one joint model would face misaligned calendars), features z-scored on the training window only, quantile edges and template initialisation likewise strictly before the prediction date. **What decides whether it is interesting**: regimes add information beyond GARCH only if the regime-conditional *means* differ - the variance part is volatility clustering, which GARCH already captures. So the row ships with two ablation controls tracked as rows of their own: the same HMM with means forced to zero (variance-only) and a single Gaussian (K = 1, the homoskedastic random walk), and it is judged by a proper scoring rule (mean log-score of the bin probabilities, with a bootstrap interval against GARCH) before hits or paper P&L are read; tuning on P&L of the fixed rule would quietly turn the row back into the allocator. **Hyperopt knobs**: regime range (2-6) and complexity penalty, validation slice length and reselection cadence, expanding vs rolling window, template smoothing rate, feature toggles (returns only / plus volatility / plus momentum) and lookbacks, covariance type and floor, optional Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage of each regime covariance; the bin count K stays fixed because it is the game. **Dependencies**: `hmmlearn` (0.3.3, not installed yet, limited-maintenance mode; set `covariance_type="full"`, iterations, `min_covar` and restarts explicitly - its defaults, diagonal covariance and 10 EM iterations, would silently change the model) and `scipy.linalg.sqrtm` for the closed-form Wasserstein distance (no optimal-transport library needed). Compute is seconds per refit at this size; template edge cases the paper leaves open (several regimes mapping to one template, a template receiving none) need a rule, e.g. one-to-one assignment when counts agree and a distance threshold that spawns a template otherwise.
- **Honest reading of that paper, so its numbers stay out of this README as expectations**: it is a single-author preprint (v1, no venue, no code, no data statement) that gives no numeric value for any hyperparameter, no tickers, no test-window dates and no significance test. Its headline Sharpe 2.18 vs 1.59 (equal weight) and drawdown -5.43% vs -14.62% (S&P 500) are portfolio-allocation metrics, gross of transaction costs and with a zero risk-free rate, over an undated out-of-sample window that its own tables and figures pin to about 680 trading days (roughly June 2023 to February 2026) - a window without 2008, 2020 or 2022 and coinciding with a strong gold rally; the strategy's average book (29% dollar proxy, 22% gold, 22% bonds, 0.4% oil, 26% equities) means a static low-volatility tilt is never ruled out as the explanation, and its own cumulative-return figure shows the S&P 500 falling about 19% where its table prints -14.62%. With 2.7 years of data the standard error of an annualised Sharpe is about 0.6, so the reported gap is about one standard error. None of that touches the regime-detection idea, which is standard and reproducible; it is why the row above is imported as a design and validated here against GARCH and the controls, not taken as a result.
- **Target - the lottery analogy made concrete**: discretize each instrument's next-day return into K quantile bins (e.g. 10, fitted on the training window only); a day's "draw" is then the vector of bin indices across instruments, i.e. a *positional* game (instrument = position, bin = digit) - exactly the pick3/Joker+ machinery (`is_positional_game`, per-position boosting, positional meta-learner, per-position DL heads). Direction (up/down) is the 2-bin special case.
- **Scoring**: per-position hits (bin exact / adjacent) plus a *paper P&L* of a fixed rule (long when the predicted bin is above the median, otherwise flat, minus a realistic fee) - the "profit" role the payout tables play for keno/pick3. Statistically the bar is the efficient-market prior: a synthetic geometric-random-walk control with matched volatility (the market equivalent of the synthetic fair-draw control), shuffled-history control, and an untouched holdout period, before any accuracy above 1/K is read as predictability.
- **Reuse**: `DataLoader`/`Backtester`/hyperopt scripts gain a market-backed loader; models stay untouched (they only see integer sequences). The RL row becomes meaningful again here (position sizing is a real player lever, unlike Joker+ digits).

**M5 - the week game (built 2026-10-04).** The owner's ask: a hold planned at the open rather than decided one morning at a time, which needs a forecast over more than one day. Every row predicts the next draw after the newest one, so a longer horizon cannot be a rolling five-day return - on a morning the newest settled five-day window ended days ago and the machinery would predict a window that is already half over. It is therefore a **second game per market with one draw per week**: `cryptoweek` and `sharesweek` (`src/MarketGame.weekly_returns`, `MarketData.WEEK_MARKETS`), the week's log return per instrument (the sum of its aligned daily returns) cut into ten quantile bins of the instrument's own past weeks (`MIN_WEEK_HISTORY` = 100 weeks before the first can be binned), the draw dated on the week's last day, and a week counting only once it is over - for crypto when the UTC week has ended (Monday 00:00 UTC), for shares from Saturday (the Friday session has closed) - and only once its last bar is in the store (`week_closed_by`: a Sunday for crypto, a Friday or the last weekday before an announced closure for shares), because a source lagging a day would otherwise make a six-day week dated a day early, re-cut the next morning, and orphan the ticket made on it; so Monday's crypto run and Saturday's shares run make the ticket for the whole coming week, a holiday Friday included, and a lagging bar costs a day, not a wrong week. The week games are games like the others: `Predictor.py` runs every row on them (a new draw once a week, so the heavy rows cost a day's work once a week; the first run back-builds the same 31 draws as for a new market, which for a week game are 31 weeks - seven months of weekly tickets, so the weekly books start with a history), the day files and the game view exist for them, and `src/MarketSettle.py` settles and exports them like a market - their instruments are rows of their own in the store (a week's result never collides with a day's), with their prices read through the base market's instrument (`MarketData.base_instrument_id`), and the open-to-close book's window is the week's first session to its last close (`ledger`'s `session` spans from the game day before - the game's own calendar from the store, not the previous settled day, so an unsettled day never stretches a daily position over two sessions). The weekly returns are the week game's returns file, so the GARCH, Regime HMM and RL Position rows run on weekly returns with their daily knobs - crude, said here, not hidden. On the page the week game is the **Week ahead** card under *Today's plan*: per instrument the best weekly book's call (Monday open to Friday close for shares, the weekly round trip for crypto; a book needs five positions, else the best exact rate), the band at the week's close as prices and as a move, how far the week has gone from the daily closes so far and whether that is inside the band, and the orders in Belgian time - for shares a market order before Monday's open and one in the last minutes before Friday's close, for crypto one at the week's start and one at the UTC week's close - with the buy cell knowing how far the week has gone (mid-week it says the book's position has been open since Monday and a buy now runs only to the close); the week's call is judged once, at the close, the card carries the market under the same rule next to the followed book and says a book followed from five positions is too few to tell from luck, and both cards keep the two games apart in words (a daily down call is not a sell for a week's hold). Two reader personas and a code reviewer went over it on 4 Oct 2026. The week games joined the tuners, the controls and the meta-learner tables on 8 Oct 2026 (M4, third part).

**M4, second part - position sizing, and the meta-learners and the ensemble tuner on the markets (built 2026-10-04).** The RL row of a market game is not a ticket builder - every row already names a bin per instrument and there is no payout table - it is the **`RL Position Model`** (`src/RLPositionModel.py`): per instrument and day it chooses a position size from 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5 or 2 times the paper stake, from what the other rows said about the day, all in bin space so the features mean the same for every instrument - the share of the voters calling the upper half and the mean of their bins around the middle (the `WeightedEnsemble Model`'s voters since 25 Oct 2026, below), the GARCH, Regime HMM and WeightedEnsemble bins, the instrument's realised bin of the newest settled day and how wild its last five days were. A linear softmax policy over the sizes, REINFORCE with a per-state baseline, trained on the market's own stored day files (the rows made for a day and the day's real bins, the real returns from the market's returns file; a history rebuild's `cutoffDate` keeps later days out), warm-started from `data/models/rl_position/<market>_position_policy.json`, wall-clock capped like the RL Ticket Model. The reward is a concave utility of the paper money the position made under the daily rule, money − `rlPositionRiskAversion` × money² (default 0.1): expected money is linear in the size, so a risk-neutral learner would only ever choose 0 or the maximum, and the quadratic term is what makes a middle size the right answer for an uncertain call. Paper only, and said so everywhere: selling what one does not own needs a margin or derivatives account, so *Today's plan* names the paper short on a down call and gives no order. On 4 Oct 2026 (the owner: "as a human I want to buy it now and place the sell order when the next condition is true") a third rule joined for the shares, **open to close** (`MarketSettle.ledger` `open`, exported for `OPEN_RULE_MARKETS` only - crypto's UTC day opens at the previous close, so its open book would repeat the daily one): the same calls, the stake bought at the session's own open and sold at its close, a fee on each leg - the one rule a reader of the page can follow, since the ticket is up hours before New York opens; its market line buys every share at the open every day, and the models table carries its total. The same day the pages were reshaped for a reader: they open with a **Today's plan** card that turns the best book's calls (open to close for shares, the daily book for crypto) into the two orders that match how the page scores a call - for a share a market order placed before the open and a market order in the last minutes before the close (or an at-the-close order where a broker offers one), with the Belgian hours for that date; the sell is at the close whatever the price - the band only says when the model counts as right - and "up" is the books' definition, a bin in the upper half; the followed book needs ten positions (`MIN_PLAN_TRADES`), else the best exact rate is followed and the card says so (a session already running or closed is said); for a coin "buy now, sell at the close of the UTC day" - the band the close must land in, how many models call it up, and when a position would be held instead (while the next morning's call is up again, which is the hold book; there is no multi-day forecast, so a hold is decided one morning at a time). The Paper trading card and a chart per instrument follow, and the six supporting cards (the explainer, the models table, accuracy per day, day by day, the proper score, the regime reading) sit as sections inside one collapsed *Background* card. `Predictor.py` treats a market like a lottery game whose CSV it downloads: where the lottery fetches, a market calls `MarketGame.daily_refresh` (bars from the sources, bins re-cut, yearly files rewritten; a source that does not answer leaves the history a day short and the run predicts from what is on disk), then predicts, and after the performance report `src/MarketSettle.py` settles every stored day file against the real returns - per model and instrument: exact bin, adjacent bin, direction (the sign of the predicted bin's representative return against the real return) and the paper P&L of the fixed rule with the real return and a 0.1% fee - into the `results` and `predictions` tables, rewrites `data/markets/<market>/results-<year>.csv` (the tracked text record; the store itself is gitignored) and writes `data/markets/<market>.json`, which the two new pages **Crypto** and **Shares** in the top navigation draw (`markets.js`): per model the rates against chance (10% exact, 28% adjacent, 50% direction) and the P&L, the accuracy per day, and per instrument the actual close with the predicted course of the three best models (the previous close moved by the predicted bin's middle return, day by day) and the next day's prediction of every model as a price with its interval. `MarketsDaily.py` is the hand-run refresh (it takes `process.lock`, since it rewrites files the predictor reads). On its first run for a market `Predictor.py` back-builds the last 31 game days as it does for any new game, with the statistical, boosting and meta rows for every day and the deep-learning rows on the newest day only (a deep-learning child costs ten to twenty minutes a day; 31 of them per market would have held the daily slot for the better part of a day), so the pages show a month of settled results from the first morning and the heavy rows accrue from there. The weekly statistical tuner names the market games to the Backtester so their tickets stay in drawn order (unnamed, it would have sorted them and then scored slots), and skips the lottery fetch for them - both found in review, as was the History page's portfolio control, which now scores a market ticket slot by slot like everything else; the fetcher refuses an empty game name outright, because the all-games endpoint would have appended every lottery's draws to the market files. Self-checks `python3 -m src.MarketGame` and `python3 -m src.MarketSettle` and `node test/markets.test.js` are in `npm test`. Two consequences worth knowing: the weekly tuners and the meta-learner retrain pick the two markets up automatically from `GAME_CONFIG` once their history exists in production, and the History page lists them as games; the control experiments include them since M3 (above), with a geometric random walk as a market's synthetic history. Sentiment feeds stay an indicator idea (above), not a row.

**M1 - the data layer (built 2026-09-30).** `src/MarketData.py` holds the store, the sources, the incremental update and the checks; `FetchMarkets.py` is the command (`--init`, `--update [--market]`, `--check`, `--status`, `--set-source`); `python3 -m src.MarketData` is the self-check (in `npm test`, no network). Three of the design's assumptions met reality that day. **Stooq is gone as a script source**: its CSV URL now serves a JavaScript browser check, whatever the client claims to be. **Yahoo answered 429 to everything** from this address, including the cookie endpoint its crumb dance starts with; the client is implemented and fixture-tested but sits as an alternate. **Nasdaq's own endpoint works keyless** (`api.nasdaq.com/api/quote/<symbol>/historical` with browser-like headers): ten years of sessions in one answer, 2,513 rows for NVDA and ASML, and it is the shares source in use. Binance behaves as designed - paginated by 1,000, the running day's candle returned open and skipped until it closes - and the first fetch stored 15,224 crypto bars in twelve seconds with no gaps. **The universe was frozen at kickoff** in the `instruments` table (`install_universe` adds what is missing and never changes a row): crypto BTC, ETH, BNB, XRP, SOL - the five largest non-stablecoins on CoinGecko that day, Tether at #3 and USDC at #6 skipped because a coin pegged to the quote currency has no return to predict - quoted in USDT, with SOL (listed 2020-08-11) bounding the joint history at about six years; shares NVDA, AAPL, MSFT and ASML's Nasdaq listing rather than Euronext, so the four share one calendar and one currency. A source is operational, not identity, and `--set-source` moves an instrument to another without touching its slot. **One decision revised**: `data/markets.sqlite3` is gitignored, like the Optuna `db.sqlite3`, because the daily commit is `git add -A` and a binary file that changes every day would put a megabyte a day into the history for data that one call re-fetches; the tracked record of the market predictions and results will be text exports per market and year (M2), the way the lottery games keep yearly CSVs. The `predictions`, `results` and `model_performance` tables exist with the shape the design names and stay empty until M2. Integrity per instrument: any missing calendar day is a gap for crypto, a hole longer than a long weekend for shares; non-positive or inverted prices are counted; staleness is days since the newest bar. **Split-adjusted history, found in review.** Nasdaq and Yahoo serve the whole history adjusted to the share basis of the day of the call - NVDA's 2024 bars are stored at a tenth of their traded price, AAPL's 2020 bars at a quarter - so a three-day incremental window would have spliced two price scales at the next split and handed the game a fake return of minus the log of the split ratio, with no check firing. For those sources every update now refetches the full answer (one call, about 2,500 rows) and rewrites the history in place; a uniform rescale of the overlapping bars is detected and logged as `HISTORY RESCALED`, on an as-traded source it escalates to a full refetch, and after one the bars older than the source's window are put on the new basis with the same factor - a uniform factor moves no log return, no bin and not the store's first bar, which is what keeps the cut game history stable (found in a second review: dropping those bars instead would have re-anchored the expanding window and re-cut a fifth of the shares' history). `--check` counts **jumps**, a close-to-close move beyond 35% (a 2:1 seam is 69%), as the detector of a seam that slipped through, and `--update --refetch-all` is the manual repair. A source change clears an instrument's bars because the bases differ (Alpha Vantage and Binance as-traded, Nasdaq and Yahoo adjusted) and cannot be spliced; a source symbol with an exchange suffix is refused for a USD instrument, because ASML.AS is the EUR Euronext listing and not the same instrument. The same review made the update loop survive any exception per instrument (a truncated response, an odd payload, the 29 February on which a ten-year window has no date), and gave the universe an honest audit trail: an instrument added in a later window carries its own date, never the kickoff's, and an entry whose slot is held by another symbol is reported as a conflict rather than silently ignored. The fetch takes no `process.lock` (network-bound, its own file) and is not yet a scheduled job: bars are re-fetchable in one call, so the daily fetch arrives with the daily market predictor in M2.
- **UI (decided 2026-09-30 with the owner; the home page reorganised on 2026-10-01 at the owner's request)**: two more sections in the top navigation next to the lottery predictor - *Crypto* and *Shares* - rather than a Markets tab on the History page; since 1 Oct 2026 the home page is a choice between the three kinds of sequence (*Lottery games*, *Crypto*, *Shares*) and the top bar repeats it, the former home page lives on as the lottery section's *New predictions* with the History next to it, and the market pages open with a card that turns the newest day into the worked example of how a day becomes a draw, plus a day-by-day list of the settled days as returns and bins (today in the app's market workbench, see *The new user interface*). Each shows, per instrument, a chart of the actual price course with the predicted course drawn on it (a predicted bin is a return interval, so it becomes a predicted next-day price with a band), the accuracy of the predictions estimated every day (exact bin, adjacent bin, direction) and the paper profit of the fixed rule, per model over the scored history. The lottery machinery still tracks the two markets as games on the History page; the market pages add the price view.
- **Signals, not predictors (owner's note, 2026-09-30)**: news and social-sentiment feeds - CryptoPanic (a crypto news aggregator with per-coin tags), LunarCrush (social volume and sentiment across X, Reddit, YouTube and TikTok, with a free API tier) and QuantMuse (an open MIT quant framework mixing factor models, ML and news/LLM sentiment for trading) - are a different approach: they judge whether a move is likely profitable, mostly for shares, from what people write. Here they can only ever be an *indicator*, never a predictor: a sentiment score for the day could enter as one more feature column of the meta-learner table, or weight a row's prediction, and then it is held to the same irrelevant-feature control as every other column. Nothing of this is built; the note records the idea and its place.
